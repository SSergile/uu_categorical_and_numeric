In terms of the baseline model, the raw inner product hit rate was a .093. The Cosine similarity hit rate was a .191. This is a rather large jump between the 2 methods, letting us know that song length was a rather large confounding variable. Finally, the TF-IDF cosine similarity hit rate was a .210. In an effort to outperform it, I created a model that used the content similarity to rank songs, but with an added boost from artist hotness. The idea was that if an artist is popular in the moment, people may be more inclined to listen to them even if their songs may not be the 'best' next option in their playlist. In terms of the hit rate, this model received a .206 while the baseline was a .210, so it was less successful in terms of getting hits. When adjusting the weights of artist hotness, the .206 number was unchanged, letting us know artist hotness isn’t the most important variable in the dataset.


One thing this tells us is that when it comes to listeners being likely to listen to a recommended song, artist popularity isn’t the strongest of variables. What is likely to be a stronger predictor that someone listens to a recommended song is the similarity of that song to the one the user is listening to. A popular artist may not be what a listener is looking for during a listening session.
